# ASCI Guidelines for Influencer Advertising in Digital Media

Source: Advertising Standards Council of India. Effective 14 June 2021, with Addendum I (15 July 2021) and Addendum II (17 August 2023).

## Definitions

**Influencer** — An Influencer is someone who has access to an audience and the power to affect their audiences' purchasing decisions or opinions about a product, service, brand or experience, because of the influencer's authority, knowledge, position, or relationship with their audience.

**Virtual Influencer** — Fictional computer generated "people" or avatars who have the realistic characteristics, features and personalities of humans, and behave in a similar manner as influencers.

**Material connection** — Any connection between an advertiser and influencer that may affect the weight or credibility of the representation made by the influencer. Material connection could include, but is not limited to, benefits and incentives such as monetary or other compensation, free products with or without any conditions attached (including those received unsolicited), discounts, gifts, contest and sweepstakes entries, trips or hotel stays, media barters, coverage, awards, or any family or employment relationship, etc.

**Digital media** — A means of communication that can be transmitted over the internet or digital networks, including internet posts, on-demand video, mobile, and digital TV.

## Preamble

As digital media becomes increasingly pervasive, it is critical that consumers must be able to distinguish when something is being promoted with an intention to influence their opinion or behaviour for an immediate or eventual commercial gain. Consumers viewing such messages without realising the commercial intent becomes inherently misleading, and in violation of Clause 1.4 (misleading by omission) and 1.5 (abuse trust of consumers or exploit their lack of experience or knowledge) of the ASCI Code.

## 1. Disclosure

All advertisements published by social media influencers or their representatives, on such influencers' accounts, must carry a disclosure label that clearly identifies it as an advertisement.

### 1.1 Criteria to determine if disclosure is required
(a) Disclosure is required if there is any material connection between the advertiser and the influencer.
(b) Material connection is not limited to monetary compensation. Disclosure is required if there is anything of value given to mention or talk about the advertiser's product or service — even free or discounted products/services or other perks, and even if the influencer was not specifically asked to talk about that product.
(c) Disclosures are required even if the evaluations are unbiased or fully originated by the influencer, so long as there is a material connection.
(d) If there is no material connection and the influencer is telling people about a product they bought and happen to like, that is not considered an advertisement and no disclosure is required.

### 1.2 Disclosure must be upfront and prominent so that it is not missed by an average consumer
(a) It should be placed in a manner that is hard to miss.
(b) Disclosures are likely to be missed if they appear only on an ABOUT ME or profile page, or bios, at the end of posts or videos, or anywhere that requires a person to click MORE.
(c) **Disclosure should not be buried in a group of hashtags or links.**
(d) Using a platform's disclosure tool should be considered in addition to an influencer's own disclosure.
(e) If the advertisement is only a picture or video post without accompanying text (e.g. Instagram stories, Snapchat), the disclosure label needs to be superimposed over the picture/video, clearly visible to the average consumer.
  - For videos ≤15 seconds: the disclosure label must stay for a minimum of 3 seconds.
  - For videos >15 seconds but <2 minutes: the disclosure label should stay for 1/3rd the length of the video.
  - For videos ≥2 minutes: the disclosure label must stay for the entire duration of the section in which the promoted brand or its features/benefits are mentioned.
(f) In live streams, the disclosure label should be announced at the beginning and end of the broadcast. If the post remains visible after the live stream, appropriate disclosure must be added to the text/caption.
(g) In audio media, the disclosure must be clearly announced at the beginning and end of the audio, and before/after every break.

### 1.3 The disclosure must be made in a manner well understood by an average consumer
(a) Permitted disclosure labels (one or more can be used): **Advertisement, Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift, "Paid Partnership" tag on Instagram, Affiliate, "Includes Paid Promotion" tag on YouTube.**
  - Ambiguous language such as merely thanking the brand (e.g. "thank you to Brand X") is NOT a permitted or sufficient disclosure label.
(b) The disclosure should be in English OR in the language of the advertisement itself, in a way that is easy for an average consumer to understand.

### 1.4 Virtual influencer disclosure
A virtual influencer must additionally disclose to consumers that they are not interacting with a real human being. This disclosure must be upfront and prominent.

### 1.5 Responsibility for disclosure
Responsibility for disclosure of material connection, and for the content of the advertisement, is upon both the advertiser and the influencer. Where the advertiser has a material connection with the influencer, the advertiser's responsibility is to ensure the posted advertisement is in line with the ASCI Code and its guidelines. The influencer is responsible for making the required disclosures; the advertiser shall, where needed, call upon the influencer to delete or edit an advertisement or the disclosure label to adhere to the ASCI Code and Guidelines.

## 2. Due Diligence

Influencers are advised to review and satisfy themselves that the advertiser is in a position to substantiate the claims made in the advertisement.

## Addendum I (15 July 2021) — Rebutting a claim of "no material connection"

If an influencer/advertiser disputes that a piece of communication is not an advertisement (i.e., claims no material connection), the following evidence is required to be submitted to ASCI:
1. A declaration from the advertiser, signed by a senior member of the advertiser's organisation (e.g. Marketing Head, Legal/Compliance Head, Digital Marketing Head), stating there is no material connection as on the date of the post.
2. Where the advertiser is difficult to trace, or the post features brands of multiple advertisers, proof of purchase of the featured products, provided by the influencer, would be considered adequate evidence to refute material connection.

## Addendum II (17 August 2023) — For Health and Financial Influencers

Because losses to consumers could be substantial due to improper advice, influencers in (a) Banking, Financial Services and Insurance (BFSI) and (b) Health and Nutrition products/services must be qualified to provide advice and must state such qualifications upfront in their posts.

1. **BFSI (stocks/investments):** influencer must be registered with SEBI and state their SEBI registration number with their name and qualifications. For other financial advice, the influencer must have suitable qualifications (e.g. IRDAI insurance license, CA, CS) and abide by all disclosure requirements mandated by financial sector regulators.
2. **Health and nutrition:** influencer must have relevant qualifications (medical degree, certified nurse, nutritionist, dietician, physiotherapist, psychologist, etc.) depending on the specific advice given.

Disclosure of such qualifications/registration must be prominent:
1. Superimposed on visuals prominently and upfront, or as the opening remark in videos.
2. For blogs/text-based posts: stated upfront before the consumer reads the post.
3. For podcasts/audio: called out at the beginning of the content.

Influencers must be able to provide proof of such qualifications/certifications if asked by ASCI.
